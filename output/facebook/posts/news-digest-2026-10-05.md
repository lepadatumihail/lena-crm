# Costa del Sol property, the week in five lines (draft for Mon 5 Oct 2026 09:30, week of 28 Sep – 4 Oct)

Card: `13-digest-2026-10-05.png`

Costa del Sol property, this week in five lines:

1. Congress threw out both of the government's housing decrees on Friday. RDL 26/2026 had been in force since 1 October and RDL 27/2026 since 2 October, so the automatic two-year tenancy extension, the extraordinary cap on rent updates and the new eviction protections all fell with the vote, and the previous rules apply again. (Newtral, OCU, idealista)

2. Andalusian resale asking prices set a record at €2,952/m², up 10% in a year and 1.7% in the quarter, while sales fell as buyers were priced out. Málaga is the region's most expensive province at €4,317/m², its highest reading on record. (idealista third-quarter report, via 101TV and The Olive Press)

3. Rents in Málaga province hit another record in September at €18.8/m², 5.7% above a year ago — about €1,880 a month for 100 m². Marbella and Ojén are both over €23/m², with year-on-year rises of up to 10%. (idealista, via El Español)

4. Foreign buyers paid an average €2,479/m² against €1,839 for Spanish buyers, around 35% more, and non-residents paid €3,242/m². Foreigners were 14.6% of purchases in Andalucía. (Registradores and notary data via idealista, carried by El Boletín and Diario en Positivo)

5. Buying in Málaga city now takes 54.5% of a household's disposable income — one of six Spanish capitals above 50%, against 36.9% nationally — and Tinsa has the city past its boom-era peak for the first time. (Tinsa IMIE, third quarter 2026)

If you told a landlord client last week that their tenancy had extended automatically or their rent rise was capped, that stopped being true on Friday — worth a call before anyone acts on it.

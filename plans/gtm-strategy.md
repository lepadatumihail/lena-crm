## 1. Market & ICP

- **Total Costa del Sol agents:** ~3,500–4,000 (vendor-stated, corroborated: Resales Online reports 1,300+ agencies; ~3 agents each). Order-of-magnitude, not census.
- **Addressable independent/solo segment (the beachhead):** ~**1,500–2,500** solo *autónomos* / micro-agencies (1–4 people), excluding the ~30–50 large branded houses (Engel & Völkers, Drumelia, Panorama, DM, etc.). *Modeled estimate, not a verified figure.*
- **TAM is not the constraint — reach is.** 5–20 payers is trivially available within the segment; the bottleneck is warm distribution.

**Validated top pains (in priority order):**
1. No way to track the same client/deal **across the many agencies an agent collaborates with** ← Lena's wedge.
2. Existing CRMs are too complex / built for agencies, not the phone-first solo agent.
3. Multilingual follow-up burden (28 nationalities; Dutch/Swedish/British/Belgian lead).
4. Commission-only pressure → a dropped follow-up = lost income, but pipeline discipline is entirely on the individual.

> ⚠️ The #1 pain is **inferred from market structure** (open/shared-listing model), not yet from first-person agent complaints. This is the assumption to test first (see §8).

**Willingness to pay (already-spending proof):**
- **Resales Online** MLS: **€150/mo** (or €1,500/yr) + €350–500 setup — 1,300+ agencies pay this.
- **Idealista Pro:** ~€60–300/mo for portal visibility/leads.
- **Inmovilla** (idealista-owned, the popular ES CRM): **from €79/mo**.
- Implication: a simple, mobile, single-agent product at **~€20–40/mo** sits *below one portal/MLS line-item* — "less than one Idealista line."

**Where they gather:** warm contacts → closed WhatsApp/Facebook broker-collaboration groups (where shared listings actually run) → **GIPE** (bilingual EN/ES, monthly Málaga events, CEPI cert), COAPI/ASAPI Málaga → Resales Online community (competitor-adjacent) → expat business networks (British/Dutch/Scandinavian). **Adoption is peer-endorsement, not paid funnels — ads are near-useless at this stage.**

## 3. Positioning

**Differentiation wedges, ranked:**
1. **Multi-agency consolidation (the spear tip).** The *only* claim no competitor can make from the individual agent's POV. Witei has inter-agency "circles" but buried in an agency publishing tool, not as the organizing principle.
2. **Simplicity / anti-bloat.** Real pain, but imitable and unprovable in a demo — supporting reason-to-believe, not the headline.
3. **AI multilingual follow-ups (10 languages).** The "wow" demo moment and emotional hook; a feature, not a category.
4. **Spanish-market fit** (ITP/notary reservation milestones, profit calculator, de-DE €, wa.me). Table-stakes credibility — "built for you, not ported from Florida."

**Honest weaknesses:** no email *sending* yet (drafts → copy-paste; masked by WhatsApp being the dominant channel here); no portal publishing (disqualifying for listing-holders, irrelevant for collaboration agents → segment hard to the latter); no lead-gen/IDX ("manage what you have," not "grow"); single-agent only (caps growth to 2–3 people); new/unproven/solo-founder/placeholder name.

**Internal positioning statement:**
> For independent Costa del Sol agents who juggle clients across several agencies, Lena is the personal, multilingual client-and-deal tracker that unifies *every client across every agency you work with* — unlike agency platforms like Inmovilla or Witei, it's built for you, not your office, and runs from your phone with nothing to set up.

**Candidate headlines (all sharper than the current "Your clients, properties, and offers. Made simple."):**
1. **"Every client. Every agency you work with. One place."**
2. **"You work with five agencies. Your clients live in one app."**
3. **"Stop losing deals between agencies, WhatsApps, and spreadsheets."**

The current headline leads with "properties" → pulls toward the listing/agency frame where Lena *loses*. All three alternatives pull toward the cross-agency personal-client frame where it uniquely *wins*.

**How to beat the three real threats:**
- **vs Spreadsheets/WhatsApp:** don't sell "a CRM" (they've rejected CRMs) — sell the consolidation moment + a recovered commission via "Needs attention." Use AI lead-extraction to import their existing WhatsApp/portal text → near-zero switching cost.
- **vs Witei free:** "Witei manages your agency's listings. Lena manages *your* clients across all the agencies you work with." Win on opinionated simplicity, not breadth.
- **vs Inmovilla/Resales:** never head-on — "Keep Resales for inventory. Lena is the personal layer it doesn't give a solo *autónomo*." Price below one MLS line so it's an *add*, not a *replace* decision.

## 4. Pricing & packaging

> **Update 2026-09-06 — Free + Pro.** Model changed to a **Free tier capped at 3 clients** (buyers + sellers together; inactive rows count; nothing is ever deleted, only *adding* a 4th client is blocked) and **Pro at €29/mo + IVA**, unlimited. **No time trial** — a usage cap converts exactly when the product is working, which suits a CRM better than a 7/14-day clock. Founding stays as the Pro discount: **first 20 paying agents lock €19/mo (or €180/yr) for life** — public, anyone who pays while spots remain, with a spots-left counter on the pricing page. Yearly at ≈20% off (€276 standard / €180 founding). Referral reward unchanged (1 free month each side on the referred agent's 2nd paid invoice); a referrer without a subscription gets a comped Pro month from the backend, a subscriber gets a balance credit. Decision record + change list: `plans/free-pro-pricing.md`. The original paid-only reasoning is kept below for history.

**Model: paid-only, 14-day no-card trial, sold as a capped "Founding Agent" cohort.** *(superseded, see above)* Charging *is* the validation — the one thing to learn is "will a Marbella agent put a card down?" Freemium optimizes for the wrong number (signups, not payers) and adds a free tier to build/gate/support with no viral loop to justify it.

**Price: €19/mo founding (locked for life) · €29/mo standard.** Annual = €190 / €290, marketed as **"2 months free"** (not "17% off").
- *Competitor anchor:* ES solo CRMs cluster €25–29; Inmovilla €79 reads "expensive/team." €29 is credible-not-cheap, €19 visibly a discount.
- *ROI anchor:* CdS commissions 3–6% on €1M+ deals; even a €4–8k collaboration slice dwarfs the price. Price should *never* be why a warm contact says no — you're buying learning + testimonials + referrals, not margin.

**Packaging: ONE plan, full product, nothing gated.** Founding extras = badge, price locked for life, direct WhatsApp line to founder, roadmap influence. The only future second tier is **Pro/rentals at €39–49/mo**, built *after* rentals ships and ≥1 founder asks to pay for it.

**Trial/onboarding mechanics:**
- 14 days, **no card** (beats 30-day by ~20%; 7 is too short to load real clients). With only a few warm contacts you can't afford door-step friction — the founder, not a paywall, is the conversion mechanism.
- **Concierge onboarding (Superhuman-style), 30–45 min 1:1:** import their cross-agency clients live + draft one AI follow-up in a buyer's language *on the call* so they feel the magic before hanging up. Biggest activation+retention lever.
- Two *human* touchpoints, not emails: day-7 check-in, day-12/13 personal WhatsApp ("trial ends Friday — switch you to Founding?").
- Close at day 14 to €19 lifetime-locked; make the discount contingent on a light commitment (testimonial or monthly 30-min feedback call). Each founder gets a personal invite link.

**Avoid:** per-seat pricing; a free tier without a hard cap (3 clients, decided 2026-09-06); card-up-front at this stage; discounting beyond the cohort (cap the lifetime lock hard at ~20); treating 5–20 agents as a quantitative A/B test (it's qualitative). **If rebranding, do it BEFORE the cohort locks in** so evangelists market the final name.

**6–12 month price path:** Months 0–3 founding cohort open (€19, no card, concierge each) → Months 3–6 close founding publicly ("spots full"), new signups to standard €29 (the closed €19 makes €29 feel earned) → Months 6–12 introduce Pro €39–49 as rentals/email-sending land; founders never move (the whole point of the lock).

## 5. Launch: invite-only verdict

**Hybrid "Founding Agents of the Costa del Sol": by-invitation framing, immediate access, paid from day one. NOT a public waitlist with a counter. NOT fully open.**

- **Why not a public waitlist:** with near-zero installed base, a "7 people waiting" counter signals weakness in a market where everyone talks; waitlists also carry a ~3× conversion penalty. Invite-only here is a **bandwidth gate** (solo founder, hands-on onboarding, hardening product) — the one legitimate reason to gate — not a hype queue.
- **Why invitation framing works:** in luxury/HNW real estate, exclusivity *is* the native language. A Founding Agent badge + "did you hear who got in" chatter travels faster than any ad.
- **The Clubhouse trap:** exclusivity attracts but doesn't retain, and **fake scarcity is lethal in a gossipy market** — if you claim "10 seats" but everyone knows you have 6 customers, the talk turns against you. **Only claim scarcity that is literally true.** The real scarce resource is your *onboarding slots*, not artificial seat caps. If 30 want in, run a second cohort.

**First 30-60-90 days:**
- **0–30 — seed cluster (3–5 design partners):** pitch each warm contact personally; recruit a **collaborating cluster** (agents who already co-broke with each other) so the network tips together; charge from day one; concierge-onboard each.
- **30–60 — tighten then expand by cohort (Linear-style):** onboard 3–5, watch what breaks, fix, invite the next 3–5. Never onboard 15 into the same bug. Ship 1–2 visible "you asked, I shipped" improvements → turns pilots into evangelists.
- **60–90 — convert & reinforce:** confirm week-2+ usage; anchor conversion on "one dropped follow-up = one lost commission"; host a small **Founding Agents dinner** in Marbella to onboard a cluster + let members vouch in person; set a hard sunset on the closed phase once warm leads are queued.

## 6. Referral program

**The core unlock is a reframe, not a bribe.** The "why arm a rival?" objection is beaten by *who* you refer: Lena's wedge is consolidation across the agencies you **collaborate** with — so the natural referral target is your **co-brokerage partners**. When they're on Lena too, *your shared deals get cleaner* (consistent records, legible offer/reservation milestones, fewer "who told the client what" mix-ups). Zero-sum fear → positive-sum: *"I want the people I do deals with on this."* No other CRM can make this move.

**Design:**
- **Two-sided give-get: 1 free month each**, triggered only when the referred agent becomes a *sustained payer* (month 1–2 complete) — never on signup. Free-tier referrers get a comped Pro month from the backend instead of a credit (2026-09-06).
- **No cash** (cheapens premium positioning, invites gaming in a small market).
- **Status is the real reward:** Founding badge + locked price.
- ~~**Each Founding Agent gets 3 personal invite codes** → limited codes make each invite a status gift, route naturally to collaborators, and keep scarcity *real*.~~ *(2026-09-06: every agent gets one permanent referral link; scarcity lives in the 20 founding spots, shown publicly.)*
- **No public leaderboards** (exposes advocates' networks to poaching).

**The flywheel:** curated entry → status chatter → invites flow to collaborators → cluster adopts together → shared deals get cleaner → retention + more invites. Keep it **manual and founder-driven** until PMF is clear — referrals amplify existing word-of-mouth, they can't manufacture it.

## 7. Naming

**Verdict: replace "Lena."** Strong placeholder, weak product name:
- **Direct collision:** `lenaai.net` is a live AI real-estate CRM for individual agents with WhatsApp automation — almost the exact product *and* name.
- **Crowded "Len-/AI" cluster** (Leena AI, asklena.ai, Leni) → dilutes premium positioning, EUIPO Class 9/42 opposition risk.
- **Domain dead-end:** lena.com/.io/.app, getlena, uselena, heylena all taken; only lenacrm.com / trylena.com remain (both weak; one keystroke from the competitor).

**Shortlist (domains verified live 2026-06-24):**

| Name | Style | Idea | Domain status | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Marlumo** ⭐ | Brandable | *mar* (sea) + *lumo* (light) | **marlumo.com AVAILABLE ($11.25)** | No built-in meaning (fix w/ tagline) |
| **Claveo** 🥈 | ES-flavoured | from *clave* (key) — "key to every client" | claveo.com taken; **getclaveo.com / claveohq.com available** | faint "Claro" echo |
| Caleta | Evocative | sheltered cove → one safe harbour | **getcaleta.com available** | common noun, soft-c mispronunciation |
| Lindero | Functional | property boundary | **getlindero.com available** | opaque to non-ES expats |
| Marea | Evocative | tide → rhythm of follow-ups | marea.es taken | ES "me da marea" = nausea |
| Faro | Functional | lighthouse → "Needs attention" beacon | exact taken (needs variant) | Algarve city + crowded |

**Top pick: Marlumo** — only candidate that clears all three constraints at once: collision-free (invented → defensible EUIPO mark, no Len-cluster dilution), **clean exact-match .com**, stays Mediterranean+premium. Cost (no built-in meaning) is cheap to fix with one tagline ("Light on every client") and irrelevant during validation.
**Runner-up: Claveo** — more instantly meaningful, confidently Spanish; drops only because the exact .com is gone.

**Pre-commit checklist:** (1) buy the domain bundle (.com + .es + .io/.app + typos); (2) **EUIPO eSearch Class 9 + 42 knockout** (this is the real gate — EU product, EUIPO > USPTO; get counsel sign-off); (3) brand-collision Google/app-store pass against incumbents; (4) lock @handles; (5) **pronunciation test with 3 real agents over a noisy WhatsApp call** — can they spell it back and say it in ES *and* EN first try?

## 8. The one thing to validate first

**Riskiest assumption:** that cross-agency client fragmentation is felt as an *acute, switch-worthy* pain — not a tolerable annoyance already absorbed into WhatsApp + memory. The entire positioning + name lean on it, but it's currently inferred from market structure, not from agent complaints.

**Test in the first 5 interviews — ask them to pull up their actual current system and count:**
> *"How many agencies did you collaborate with on deals in the last 6 months, and where do those clients live right now?"*

- **"3+ agencies, and it's a mess across WhatsApp/notes"** → wedge holds → sell hard on consolidation, commit the landing page + name to it.
- **"1–2, I manage fine"** → pivot the spear to **multilingual AI follow-ups + Needs-Attention** as the lead, demote consolidation to a feature.

**Do this before committing the landing headline and the new name.**
